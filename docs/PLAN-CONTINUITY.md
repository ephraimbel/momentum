## What the implementation covers

- `SchemaV8` adds `PlanContinuityRecord`, a scalar-keyed sidecar. Released profile/plan shapes and historical schema versions remain frozen. The sidecar owns illness state, the post-interruption evidence boundary, cloud revision, a durable outbox, conflict copies, and the previous local snapshot.
- `PlanCloudSnapshot` encodes actual plan identity, prescribed sessions, completion links, profile inputs, explicit time limits, benchmark provenance, pace/threshold state, saved plans, season/events and decision sidecars. Restoration preserves the selected plan rather than rerunning generation.
- Momentum-owned workout summaries from the last 90 days, plus older workouts linked to active/saved plan completions, supply adaptation evidence. Exercise definitions and logged strength sets accompany referenced history. Existing local workouts are retained, including their local edits and raw samples.
- This is **plan continuity, not complete workout-history synchronization**. It does not transfer GPS samples, maps, photos, meals, awards, or HealthKit history. Local workout edits win when a summary has the same ID. There is no cross-device deletion/tombstone protocol for workout history; do not describe it as one.
- Snapshot version, size (8 MiB), identities, references, dates and numeric prescriptions are validated before restoration. Oversized/unsupported data produces a visible sync error and preserves the local plan; there is no silent truncation.
- `PlanSyncService` binds the cloud owner to the current local email/Google/Apple identity. It rechecks the owner and local graph after awaits, defers restoration during recording, and checks cloud restoration before starting onboarding on a fresh signed-in device.
- Writes use a persisted operation ID and server-side expected revision. Lost acknowledgements can be retried; conflicting edits require an explicit choice. Journal saves coalesce without cancelling their own network task. Unresolved conflicts do not continuously poll.
- Choosing either conflict copy or restoring the retained previous copy preserves active injury reports and the stricter illness state. A clean, ordered cloud update can legitimately complete recovery. Previous-copy restoration rereads the server revision before replacing anything. Both choices union completed training evidence from both copies. A completed session keeps its original prescription, date and workout link even when restoring an older plan. Completed extra sessions remain when restoring the same plan identity. Existing local workout edits still win on the device.
- Explicit local erasure suspends sync and persists fresh-setup intent across relaunches without silently restoring the erased plan. An interrupted erase persists a device-wide block: no account may restore or upload the partly erased store. A dedicated Finish device reset action completes deletion before setup opens. Account aliases share fresh-setup intent through the cloud owner; completing setup clears it for every known alias. Account switching invalidates in-flight work. Account deletion removes the server snapshot through the auth-user foreign-key cascade, then clears local cloud ownership while retaining device training if requested.

## Illness return policy

The self-guided path is scoped to mild respiratory symptoms. Other/uncertain illness, prolonged illness, concerning symptoms, or advice to avoid exercise require clinician advice before return. It does not diagnose illness, certify race readiness, or promise a recovery date.

The [IOC respiratory-illness consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35863871/) supports a graded exercise challenge, checking responses during/after exercise and at 24 hours, and stopping/reviewing abnormal responses. The [CDC respiratory-virus guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/respiratory-viruses/prevention/precautions-when-sick.html) describes improving symptoms and 24 hours fever-free without fever-reducing medication for return to normal activities; **that is not clearance for sport**.

The product rules are deliberately explicit: rest has no automatic expiry; the first outing is capped at 15 easy minutes; progression requires a real retained outing, at least 24 hours, and positive response feedback. The building stage uses easy sessions no longer than 30 minutes or 70% of their prior dose, whichever is smaller, with at least 24 hours between credited outings. Rebuilding requires at least seven days and two retained easy outings on separate days, each with a response window. These exact caps, the five-minute evidence minimum, seven-day interval and two-outing count are **initial conservative product heuristics, not clinically validated prescriptions**. Clinical/product review remains a release requirement, especially for severe illness and athletes with complex conditions.

Rebuilds, availability edits and undo retain restrictions. Missed illness sessions retire without make-up debt. Races keep their date but cannot start through the planned-session launch while return restrictions are active. Ordinary training/race/catch-up reminders are cleared, and phone/watch/widget eligibility follows the same restrictions. A separate free workout remains possible; the app provides guidance rather than claiming medical control.

After an interruption, volume estimates exclude pre-illness runs and count untrained days until the evidence window has rolled forward. Deleted outings cannot unlock progression or permanently prevent retrying the first easy outing. Onboarding now distinguishes new, regular, consistent and returning runners. Returning runners can explicitly enter zero running in the last four weeks; draft restoration keeps their choice, and old peak mileage is not revived. The animated reveal and App Store review beat remain in place.

Injury restrictions are also reapplied to newly generated or restored sessions at the shared save boundary. Repeated saves do not compound the same reduction. The check-in date is not automatic clearance. A scheduled race retains its identity, date and distance during an injury; the shared phone/Today/watch eligibility check prevents starting it while restrictions apply. Ordinary plan undo cannot clear a later injury report.

## Verified engineering behavior

- SwiftData restoration reconciles the existing plan/session/exercise graph in place. The previous delete-and-reinsert approach crashed in three regression fixtures. Restoration, both conflict choices, and failed-save rollback now pass, including preservation of original workout data.
- A real onboarding-generated race plan with supporting strength, exercise references and running sidecars restores into an existing second store and stabilizes across repeated restores. Its unchanged snapshot does not produce endless sync revisions.
- HTTP transport fixtures inspect actual URLSession requests, owner filtering, Authorization, expected revision, operation ID and snapshot/date roundtrips. A 401 is an authentication error, never interpreted as an empty cloud plan.
- Athlete-wide recovery changes invalidate existing proposals even when session prescriptions are unchanged. Moving this check into the existing athlete-context fingerprint removed the redundant profile fetch from every plan fingerprint: 20 long-plan fingerprints fell from about 320 ms to 24–27 ms, with the original 200 ms test ceiling retained.
- Ten isolated PostgreSQL checks pass: anonymous denial, first save, exact retry, mismatched operation content, stale revision, direct-write denial, owner isolation, malformed/oversized payloads and account-deletion cascade.
- On the linked live backend, catalog checks verified RLS enabled, authenticated SELECT/RPC access, no direct writes, and no anonymous table/RPC access. Tests used two temporary auth identities inside a transaction, verified owner isolation, CAS, retry identity and deletion cascade, then rolled everything back. A separate read confirmed **zero continuity rows and one migration record** afterward. Dashboard/API requests transiently failed before succeeding; only verified successful results are counted.
- Simulator UI checks cover returning-runner selection and zero-volume entry, regular/Reduce Motion onboarding, editable benchmark entry, time limits, generated reveal after backgrounding, saved plan drafts, Manage Plan apply/undo, and illness pause → relaunch → shared check-in. All four existing App Store review-page tests pass, including reveal → review and an always-available Continue button.
